# Research reuse

Use this reference when the user asks for comparison, review synthesis, experiment planning, or citation-ready writing from existing notes.

## Research-translation chain

Build one connected chain before offering additional ideas:

1. **Reusable design** — name the paper mechanism, measurement, dataset construction, argument move, or evaluation setup worth carrying forward.
2. **Dependent assumption** — state the condition on which that design or the paper's conclusion depends.
3. **Testable experiment** — test that assumption rather than merely “use more data”. Specify:
   - `intervention`: what changes;
   - `comparison`: control, baseline, counterfactual, or alternative;
   - `metrics`: measurements and direction/threshold of interest;
   - `decision`: what result would retain, revise, or reject the design/claim.
4. **Follow-up question** — identify the next paper, evidence, or boundary needed after the decision.

Label new steps as `reader_inference` or `research_proposal`; never present them as author conclusions.

## Reusing the library

Start from notes to locate candidate claims and typed relations. Return to the Zotero PDF for important quotations, numerical claims, disputed boundaries, or any content whose anchor is missing. Cite the paper, not the generated note, in external writing.

For comparisons, align research question, population/data, mechanism, evidence design, metric, and conclusion boundary before synthesizing. For reviews, organize by claims and relations rather than listing papers. For writing, separate supported statements from interpretive synthesis and unresolved evidence gaps.

Stop when the available notes and PDFs cannot support the requested claim. Report the missing evidence instead of producing citation-shaped speculation.
